# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary users are WebMCP Challenge judges operating the product directly in
ChatGPT's in-app browser or a WebMCP-enabled Chrome session. The same experience
must also communicate a credible, reusable product concept to future customers,
partners, and browser-platform builders.

Users need to understand the current plan, collaborate with their browser agent,
inspect what the agent changed, retain authority over consequential actions, and
see reliable evidence of execution and recovery.

## Product Purpose

Create a polished reference experience for the agent-native web. The product
demonstrates how a human and an external browser agent can operate one live,
visible decision surface through WebMCP while deterministic application logic
preserves state, validation, authorization, and execution integrity.

Success means judges can operate the complete experience without explanation,
recognize non-trivial WebMCP leverage, and leave convinced that the implementation
both pushes the current API and reveals useful directions for future versions.

## Brand Personality

Precise, quietly ambitious, trustworthy.

The product should feel controlled and technically confident. Its ambition comes
through the interaction model and completeness of execution, not promotional
claims or visual spectacle.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI dashboards built from interchangeable cards and oversized metrics.
- Chatbot-first layouts that reduce the product to a conversation transcript.
- Dark neon agent consoles, terminal cosplay, and science-fiction decoration.
- Excessive cards, nested containers, glass effects, and decorative gradients.
- Hackathon-demo roughness, placeholder copy, unexplained controls, or staged
  interactions that do not survive direct use.

## Design Principles

1. Practice the protocol: every important agent action must produce an immediate,
   legible change in the same surface the human operates.
2. Make authority visible: distinguish inspection, proposal, authorization, and
   execution without relying on explanatory prose.
3. Demonstrate depth through behavior: state-aware tools, stale-state rejection,
   recovery, and receipts matter more than a large tool catalog.
4. Let the product prove the thesis: use restrained language and make technical
   completeness directly observable.
5. Expose the frontier honestly: identify where current WebMCP behavior is strong
   and where future protocol capabilities would improve the experience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal accessibility conformance target is required for this hackathon demo.
The product will still preserve baseline keyboard usability, visible interaction
states, readable contrast, reduced-motion respect, and status communication that
does not depend on color alone as part of normal product quality.
